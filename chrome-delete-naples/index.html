A chrome delete is the highest-leverage small job in wrapping: window surrounds, grille trim, mirror caps, badges and roof rails go dark, and a car you have seen every day suddenly has intent. Zap Wrap Naples runs deletes with the same discipline as full exotic builds: film wraps around the piece, never cut against your paint.

What goes dark, and how.

Scope is per piece, and the usual list looks like this:

  • Window surrounds and beltline trim
  • Grille frame and inserts, where the mesh allows film
  • Mirror caps
  • Badges and lettering
  • Roof rails and door pillars
  • Fog bezels and lower brightwork

Where a piece unclips, it comes off the car and gets wrapped on the bench: film around every edge, heat-set corners, then back on the car. Where the factory glued it in place, we wrap it in position with the edge tucked, and we tell you which pieces those are before we start. What you will not find here is film knifed on top of your paint, or black tape pretending to be a wrap.

One honest note: raw textured plastic does not bond film cleanly. If part of your trim is that plastic, you hear it at the inspection, not in August when an edge lets go.

Finishes for a delete.

Satin black is the delete: the blackout look the factories sell as an option package, done in film on the car you already own. The codes we reach for most:

  • Satin black: 3M S12, Avery Dennison SW900-197-O
  • Gloss black: 3M G12, Avery Dennison SW900-190-O, KPMF K75443 Gloss Perfect Black
  • Matte black: Avery Dennison SW900-180-O, KPMF K75547 Matte Perfect Black
  • Accent: 3M CFS12 Black Carbon Fiber on mirror caps and spoilers

Same cast-film catalogs as our full wraps: no economy trim rolls. And if the car keeps its factory color, half the consult is matching the black's sheen to your paint's temperature: satin against matte-adjacent colors, gloss against deep metallics.

Brightwork versus the Gulf.

Trim has a hard life on this coast. Salt air works on chrome and anodized brightwork from the day a car arrives in Naples: haze first, pitting later, and no polish brings pitted trim back. Film gets there first: the piece goes dark, and the metal underneath stops aging. If the truck tows to the ramp at Caxambas or the Naples city dock, that math doubles.

And this is a town where the blacked-out G-Wagon is a daily: against that backdrop, stock chrome reads unfinished. Cars come to the Naples bay for deletes from Bonita Springs, Marco Island, Fort Myers and Cape Coral: it is a short stay, and it changes the whole car.

Chrome delete questions.

Can I get the chrome back later?

Yes. The film peels and the factory brightwork comes back the way we found it: that is the point of doing a delete in film instead of paint. The bonus nobody mentions: every year the trim spent covered, it spent protected.

Why film instead of painting or powder-coating the trim?

Painting trim properly means disassembly, prep, spray and cure, and it is permanent: change your mind later and you are buying new trim. Film does the same visual job, comes off clean, and never gambles with factory pieces. It is also days faster in and out of the bay.

Does film stick to textured plastic?

Smooth chrome, gloss plastic and painted trim: yes, beautifully. Raw textured plastic: no, and anyone who says otherwise is selling you a comeback visit. We flag those pieces at the inspection and tell you exactly what they are before we start.

Can you do just the badges or just the window trim?

Yes: scope is per piece. Plenty of owners start with window surrounds and mirrors, live with it for a week, then book the rest. The car tells you.
