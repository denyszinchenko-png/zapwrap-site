What a full color change includes.

The difference between a wrap that turns heads and a wrap that lifts at the gas station is everything that happens before film touches paint.

  • Paint inspection first. Film belongs on healthy factory paint. If your clear coat is failing or a panel has been resprayed, you hear it at the inspection, not after removal.
  • Vehicle-specific disassembly. Mirrors, handles, badges and trim are removed where appropriate and safe, so the agreed edges can be finished cleanly.
  • Plan before installation. We review the vehicle, selected finish, coverage and disassembly points before film is ordered or cut.
  • Install and heat-set. Many full color changes take about 3 to 5 working days. Vehicle condition, complexity and film availability determine the confirmed schedule.
  • Optional ceramic seal. A ceramic coat laid over the finished film: deeper look, easier washes. Add-on only, always on top of a wrap.
  • Handoff. You leave with the care card for your exact film and a car you will circle twice before driving.

Color change questions.

Can you match a color from a photo or another car?

Bring the reference. We compare it with suitable, available manufacturer colors and physical swatches whenever possible. If no stock film is close enough, we say so before quoting and discuss alternative design options.

Gloss, satin or matte: which one for Florida?

Gloss, satin and matte are available in automotive film lines, but the suitable product depends on the vehicle and intended use. Gloss reads closest to paint, satin softens glare, and matte needs finish-specific care. Existing chips, texture and bodywork can still show through any finish, so surface inspection comes first.

Does a full color change include the door jambs?

Standard scope is every exterior painted panel, with edges wrapped so open doors show clean lines and jambs left factory. Wrapping jambs means another level of disassembly: worth it on a show build or a dramatic color flip, skippable on a daily. We scope it honestly at the consult.

3M, Avery Dennison or KPMF: how do you choose?

By finish, not brand loyalty. Avery solids carry the longest maker ratings of the lines we install. 3M owns several shifts and textures nobody else makes. KPMF rolls iridescents that stop traffic. You pick the color you cannot stop thinking about: we pick the roll that installs and lasts best in that finish.
